Stone & Shore

Ordu, İstanbul, Sümela — and a quiet motto

Temet nosce Know yourself

Temet nosce. Know yourself. It sounds heavy until you say it on a road that already knows you — Black Sea wind in Ordu, a late table with friends, the kind of night that does not ask for a status update.

I keep that line close because the day job is all maps and messages: EDI lanes, APIs, systems that must agree. Off the clock, the map is simpler. Shore. Stone. People who stay.

Ordu — the shore that resets the clock

Ordu is not a postcard I perform for. It is breakfast with a view, a bench that faces the water, twilight when the day softens. When the coast is quiet, I remember I am allowed to be quiet too.

Friends here do not need the résumé version of me. Fist bumps after a night drive. Rain ponchos at a waterfall. A table that fills itself. That is the proof I trust.

İstanbul — the city on the water

İstanbul moves faster. Kız Kulesi sits in the current like a comma — a pause you notice only if you look. I like nights that keep a little noise and a little iced filtre, when the city feels close but not crowded inside your head.

Not every frame needs a caption. Some are just proof that I showed up for the hour.

Sümela — stone that remembers

Sümela Monastery does not rush you. Fresco color on cliff stone. A dome that asks you to look up. Walking through with friends turns history into company — not a lecture, a shared silence with walls that have seen more winters than we will.

Stone that remembers is a good mirror. You leave lighter if you let it.

What I keep

This notebook is not a sprint board. It is the same energy as the stories: short when it can be, longer when a place deserves room. Ordu shores. İstanbul nights. Sümela stone. Temet nosce on the way.

The road is long, but beautiful. I will keep using this place as a notebook — not too much, just enough.
