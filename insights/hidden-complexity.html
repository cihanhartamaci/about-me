The Hidden Complexity of Connecting Systems

Integration Architecture Idempotency

On paper, an integration often looks simple: receive a request, transform the data, call another API, and return a response.

In practice, the complexity lives between those steps.

What happens when the downstream service is unavailable? What if a request times out but the operation actually succeeds? What if the same event arrives twice? What if two systems interpret the same field differently?

These are not unusual edge cases. They are fundamental engineering problems.

Reliable integrations require thoughtful decisions around retries, idempotency, data validation, error handling, and system boundaries. They also require understanding the business process behind the data.

A technically valid API request can still produce the wrong business outcome.

That's why I believe integration engineering sits at the intersection of software architecture and business understanding. The best solutions don't merely move data between systems; they preserve the meaning and integrity of that data throughout the process.

Sometimes, the most important engineering work happens in the spaces between systems.
